# ADR-0022: Currency-rate cache and conversion service

Status: Accepted (2026-11-20)

## Context

FX1 needs a rate-provider port, a SQLite cache of rates with provenance, and a conversion service that reports value, provenance and age, makes missing and stale rates explicit, never invents a rate, and keeps serving the cache when the provider is down or its quota is exhausted.

Decisions in force: D5 (any ISO 4217 code; exact decimals with their own scale; half-even rounding only at presentation and conversion; per-currency totals first, a converted aggregate second, items without an acceptable rate excluded and flagged) and D11 (Frankfurter with historical access, behind an adapter; store provider, the provider/source key, the exact rate, the rate date and the retrieval time; keep cached rates during outages; never invent a rate; refresh daily; a cached rate is stale after 5 days).

Research on Frankfurter's responses (a knowledge-base proposal, not yet accepted) observed that its blended rates, its per-source routes (for example ECB) and its v1 route give different values for the same pair and date, that per-source routes answer a weekend date with the previous working day while the blended route answers with the requested date, and that rates come as JSON numbers.

## Decision

1. **Ports in `internal/domain/fx`.** `Provider` (`Name`, `Source`, `Latest`, `OnDate`) returns `Quote`s: source key, pair, exact `money.Decimal` rate, rate date. Adapters parse rates from the response text (for JSON numbers, `json.Number`), never through `float64`, and classify failures as `*ProviderError` (`rate_limited` with `RetryAfter`, `unavailable`, `permanent`); an unclassified error counts as `unavailable`. Error messages carry provider, operation, kind and code only. `Cache` (`Save`, `Find`) is implemented by `internal/store/fxrates`.
2. **Cache row** (`fx_rates`, STRICT, WITHOUT ROWID): provider, source, base and quote currency, rate as exact TEXT (positive, `sqlvalue.AmountCheck`), rate date, first and last retrieval time (UTC), `confirmed_through` (a date), and `previous_rate` with `revised_at` (both null until a revision). Key: (provider, source, base, quote, rate date). The source is part of the key because different sources give different values for the same pair and date. Rates are provider facts, unversioned, never edited through the API.
   - `confirmed_through` is the latest date a retrieval confirmed the rate applies to: the date asked for (today, for a latest retrieval), but never past the last UTC day whose publication is certainly over: the day before the one that `retrieved_at` minus 6 hours falls in. A retrieval made at 09:00 therefore cannot make its rate final for that afternoon's publication, and one made early the next day does not confirm the day just ended. The 6-hour grace covers providers whose day runs behind UTC; an adapter-declared cut-off could replace it. It is kept as the maximum on every re-retrieval of the same value and is never before the rate date.
   - A re-retrieval of the same value (compared numerically, so `1.10` and `1.1` are the same) moves `retrieved_at` and `confirmed_through` forward and keeps `first_retrieved_at`. A numerically different value is a **revision**: the new value replaces the old, the old goes to `previous_rate` with `revised_at`, `first_retrieved_at` restarts at the revision (Ledger never held the new value earlier), and the service logs a warning with provider, source, pair, rate date and old and new value. Only the last superseded value is kept, not a full history. An older retrieval changes nothing.
3. **One source per service.** The service converts with the adapter's configured source only; rates of other sources are cached but not used. An answer that holds rates but none under the configured source is a permanent `source_mismatch` error (an adapter misconfiguration), not "not published".
4. **No invented rates.** The service never inverts a pair, triangulates through a third currency, interpolates between dates or defaults to 1. A provider answer with a non-positive rate, a different base or a date after the one asked (one day of tolerance for the latest rate, since the provider's calendar may run ahead of UTC) is a permanent provider error and nothing from it is cached, and so is an answer with two different values for one source, pair and date (`invalid_rate`).
5. **Freshness** (`fx.Freshness`, defaults from D11): `RefreshAfter` 24 h, `StaleAfter` 5 days.
   - `Convert` (latest): a cached rate retrieved less than `RefreshAfter` ago is used without calling the provider. Otherwise the provider is asked; on failure the cache is served. The result is **stale** when the rate was last retrieved `StaleAfter` ago or more (`not_retrieved_recently`) or its date is more than `StaleAfter` in whole days before today, UTC (`rate_date_too_old`). Staleness is measured from retrieval, so a Friday rate confirmed on Monday is current, and ECB weekends and holidays don't trip it.
   - `ConvertOn(date)` (historical): the newest rate dated on or before the date. A cached rate is final for the date only when it is dated that day or a retrieval confirmed it through that day (`confirmed_through` is on or after the date), so it is used without a provider call. When a rate was retrieved says nothing about which dates the retrieval covered: an answer to "the rate on 5 November" does not show that nothing was published on 9 November, so it is not final for the 9th. (An earlier version treated any rate retrieved after the date as final and could answer for a later date with an older rate.) Otherwise the provider is asked. If the provider can't be reached, an earlier unconfirmed rate is reported as stale (`unconfirmed`). A gap of more than `StaleAfter` days is stale. A future date is an error, and today uses `Convert`. Historical rates don't go stale by retrieval age.
6. **Result.** `Conversion` has a status (`ok`, `stale`, `missing`) and a reason. For `ok` it carries `Value` (rounded half-even to the target's ISO 4217 minor units, or exact for a currency without them) and `Exact` (the unrounded product). It also carries `Rate` (full provenance), `Age` (since last retrieval), `FromCache` and `RefreshError`. Stale and missing results carry no value, so they can't be summed by mistake. A stale result still carries the rate, so it can be shown. Only `ok` is `Acceptable()` for converted totals (D5).
   - `Convert` (latest) also requires that the cached rate was confirmed through the last day over at retrieval time (see `confirmed_through`). That holds for a latest retrieval, and for a dated retrieval of that last finished day itself; a rate fetched for any earlier date is never served as current.
7. **Outages and quota exhaustion.** After an `unavailable` or `rate_limited` failure, provider calls pause (in memory, per service): until the provider's `RetryAfter` when it gives one, capped at `StaleAfter`, otherwise for `FailurePause` (default 5 minutes, never more than `RefreshAfter`). Conversions keep coming from the cache and report the failure in `RefreshError`, so an outage costs one provider timeout per pause, not one per conversion. A `permanent` failure pauses nothing. Concurrent conversions of the same pair and date share one provider call (single-flight).
8. **Provider calls are made outside write transactions**; `Save` writes a whole answer in one transaction after validating every rate.

## Why

- Measuring staleness from the last retrieval, not only from the rate date, keeps "the provider has been unreachable for 5 days" distinct from "the provider publishes nothing newer" (weekends, holidays). The rate-date check still catches a provider that keeps answering with an old date.
- A finality rule for dated rates avoids calling the provider every time a weekend or holiday date is converted, without assuming a holiday calendar.
- Keeping the source in the key preserves provenance when the configured route changes, and avoids mixing blended and single-source values in one series.
- Rejected: inverting a pair (1/rate needs rounding, so it would be a derived rate the provider never published); storing rates as REAL (ADR-0005); hard-coding Frankfurter's shapes in the domain (ADR-0001 boundary; FX2 owns them); a stale result with a computed value (too easy to sum by mistake).

## Consequences

- **Not in this change:**
  - The Frankfurter adapter (FX2), with its route choice (blended or a provider key such as ECB) and JSON-number parsing.
  - Configuration keys for the freshness policy and the source, which are wired with FX2.
  - The daily refresh job: `Service.Refresh` exists, but no scheduler calls it yet.
  - HTTP endpoints, OpenAPI and MCP tools. The route-access pattern (A1, PR #25) is not on `work` yet, and FX3 (outlook) is the first consumer.
  - Audit entries: no user action exists yet.
- The pause is in memory and is lost on restart. The first call after a restart may hit the provider once more.
- A provider's revision of a past rate replaces the cached value; the superseded value is kept (`previous_rate`, `revised_at`) but only one level deep, and the revision is logged. A figure that must stay reproducible (for example a reported converted total) still has to copy the rate it used: FX3 must hold to that. `Conversion.Exact` has the scale of the amount plus the rate's (up to 48), beyond the 24 the SQL amount encoding accepts: a consumer stores the rate and the rounded value, not `Exact`.
- Quotes for currencies that were not asked for are cached when a provider returns them: they are real published rates.
- **Migration numbering:** this change is `0007_fx_rates`, after A1's `0006_users` and B2's `0005_bank_refresh`, with a fixture for version 6 (`0006_users`).
- **Open:** the precision Frankfurter publishes is undocumented (about 4 to 5 decimals were observed), and rates are stored exactly as returned. Which Frankfurter route to use is an FX2 question for the operator if R7's ECB caveat matters.
